AI agents: what they are and where they fit.

AI agents are attracting attention because they can do more than respond to a single prompt. Here is a grounded way to understand the idea.

An AI agent is a software system that uses a model to pursue a defined task, often by choosing steps, calling approved tools and checking results along the way.

From answers to actions

A basic chatbot generates a response to a request. An agent-style workflow may break a goal into smaller steps—for example, finding information in approved documents, summarising it and preparing a draft for a person to review. The difference is not magic or independent judgement: it is a workflow that gives a model access to instructions, context and selected tools.

Where they may help

They can be useful when a process has clear boundaries, repeatable steps and a measurable outcome. Examples include preparing a first draft from internal material, organising support requests or helping staff locate information. The right fit depends on the quality of the data, the cost of errors and how much review is needed.

Keep people in control

Agent workflows can make mistakes, misunderstand a request or use a tool in an unexpected way. Start with low-risk tasks, limit permissions, log actions and require approval before important external or irreversible steps. A human should remain accountable for decisions that affect people, money, safety or sensitive information.

A useful starting point

Choose one real task, map the steps, identify what the system is allowed to access and define how success will be checked. Compare the assisted process with the current one before expanding it. The best automation is not the most autonomous—it is the one that reliably helps people do useful work.
