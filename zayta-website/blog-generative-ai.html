GENERATIVE AI · PRACTICAL GUIDE

Bringing generative AI into everyday work.

A thoughtful first step is not to automate everything. It is to find one well-defined task where assistance can save time without lowering quality.

Generative AI tools can help people draft, summarise, brainstorm and transform information. Their output is a starting point, not a guarantee of accuracy.

Choose a task with clear boundaries

Good early experiments are repetitive, easy to review and low-risk. A team might ask a tool to produce a first draft from non-sensitive notes or turn a long public document into a short outline. Define what the system should and should not do before inviting people to use it.

Keep expertise in the loop

AI-generated text can sound convincing while containing errors, omissions or unsupported claims. A person with the right context should verify facts, tone and completeness before the result is shared or used to make a decision. For specialised work, subject-matter review is essential.

Protect information

Before using a tool, understand its data-handling terms and your organisation’s policies. Do not paste personal, confidential or commercially sensitive information into a service unless it has been approved for that purpose. Use fictional or anonymised examples when testing.

Measure what matters

Compare the assisted task with the usual approach. Consider accuracy, time saved, review effort and the experience of the people using the result. If the tool adds more checking than value, change the workflow or choose a different use case.
