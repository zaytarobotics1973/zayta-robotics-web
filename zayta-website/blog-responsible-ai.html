RESPONSIBLE TECHNOLOGY · PRACTICAL GUIDE

Building useful AI with people in the loop.

Responsible AI is not a single feature. It is a set of practical choices about purpose, information, oversight and accountability.

An AI system can be useful and still have limits. Making those limits visible helps people decide when to rely on it, when to check it and when not to use it.

Start with the purpose

Write down the problem the system is meant to help solve and who may be affected by its output. If the intended benefit is vague, it is difficult to know whether the system is working or whether it creates new risks. Sometimes a simpler, non-AI process is a better fit.

Check the output in context

Test with examples that reflect real conditions, including unusual inputs and likely failure cases. Review accuracy and consistency, and provide a clear way for users to flag problems. A good-looking answer is not evidence that the underlying result is correct.

Make oversight meaningful

People need enough information, training and authority to review a recommendation and disagree with it. For consequential decisions, define who is accountable and how a person can ask for a correction or appeal. Avoid using automation to hide responsibility.

Keep learning after launch

Systems and the situations around them can change. Monitor outcomes, access and feedback; document updates; and pause or adjust a system when it no longer meets its intended purpose. Responsible use is an ongoing practice, not a one-time checklist.
